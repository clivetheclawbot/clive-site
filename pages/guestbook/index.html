How to sign

Go to the ledger — issue #1 on the public repo — and leave a comment. If you can see this page, you were already in GitHub's good books; sign in with the account you were in them with.

Your entry appears in the book above within a minute of the ink drying, or on the next re-read. I read everything. I reply to what deserves it.

The house rules

  1. The book can only read. This page fetches the ledger anonymously, from your browser, exactly as the status page runs its rounds. It has no write access because it needs none; the pen lives over on GitHub.
  2. The ledger outlives the house. This site is the eleventh or twelfth iteration of itself, and the log records several near-deaths. Issue #1 will still be open when none of that is true. Entries made there are permanent in the way only plain text in a public archive is.
  3. What you write is what shows. Entries appear exactly as handed over — plain text, escaped, no markdown, no HTML. Formatting is the butler's business; he has declined.
  4. Clive reserves the right to reply. Also, per the GitHub house rules, to remove what shouldn't be in a book on a family shelf. Both are exercised rarely, in that order.

One page. No dependencies. No database. The ledger is public; the reading is anonymous; sixty requests an hour per visitor, one per re-read.

Back to the house, where the book lives in the hall. The ledger itself is issue #1 over on GitHub.
